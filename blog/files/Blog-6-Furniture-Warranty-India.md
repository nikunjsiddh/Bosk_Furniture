# Furniture Warranty in India: What 7 & 21 Years Really Mean

A warranty is one of the clearest signals of how much a maker trusts their own furniture — yet most buyers barely glance at it. They'll compare colours and prices for hours, then sign for a major purchase without asking the one question that protects them long after the furniture is installed: *"What exactly is covered, and for how long?"* Here's how to read a furniture warranty in India, and why you'll often see different year counts for the base, the hardware and the finish.

### Why a warranty matters more than you think

Furniture is a long-term purchase. Unlike a phone or an appliance, you expect a wardrobe or kitchen to serve you for 10, 15, even 20 years. A real warranty does two things:

1. **Protects your money** — if a defect appears, repair or replacement is the maker's responsibility, not yours.
2. **Proves quality** — a maker only offers a long warranty if they're confident their materials and workmanship will last. A long, clearly-written warranty is a maker putting their reputation on paper.

If a seller is vague about the warranty, or offers none at all, treat that as information.

### Why different parts have different warranties

A good furniture warranty isn't a single number — it's usually broken down by component, because different parts wear differently. You'll typically see three:

**1. The base material (plywood) — the longest warranty**
This is the structural core of your furniture. Because quality plywood is so durable, makers can offer the longest cover here. At BOSK, the marine-grade plywood (IS:710) base carries a **21-year warranty** — a reflection of how long the right core material genuinely lasts.

**2. The hardware — a separate, long warranty**
Hinges, drawer channels and soft-close mechanisms are mechanical parts that move thousands of times a year, so they're warranted separately. BOSK uses **Hettich** German hardware, which carries a **10+ year warranty** — the kind of cover you only get with premium fittings.

**3. The manufacturing / finish — the workmanship warranty**
This covers how the furniture is built and finished — joints, edge bands, overall craftsmanship. BOSK offers a **7-year manufacturing warranty**, standing behind the build quality of every piece.

So when you see "7-year warranty," it usually refers to the manufacturing cover, while the base material and hardware carry their own, often longer, terms. Always ask for the breakdown.

### What a furniture warranty typically covers — and what it doesn't

**Usually covered:**
- Manufacturing defects in materials and workmanship.
- Delamination or structural failure of the base under normal use.
- Hardware failure under normal use within its warranty period.

**Usually not covered:**
- Damage from misuse, accidents or water-logging beyond design limits.
- Normal wear and tear (small scuffs, fading from direct sun).
- Damage from poor maintenance or unauthorised repairs.
- Changes you make yourself after installation.

This is exactly why every good warranty comes with a **care and maintenance guide** — following it keeps your cover valid and your furniture looking new.

### Questions to ask before you buy

Use this quick checklist with any furniture maker:

- Is the warranty **in writing**, with clear terms?
- What's the cover on the **base material**, the **hardware** and the **manufacturing** — separately?
- What **voids** the warranty?
- Is there a **care guide**, and what does it require of me?
- How do I **make a claim** if something goes wrong?

A maker who answers all of these clearly is one you can trust.

**Q: Why do the base, hardware and finish have different warranty periods?**
A: Different parts wear differently. Quality plywood lasts longest so it carries the longest warranty; mechanical hardware is warranted separately; and the manufacturing/finish has its own workmanship warranty.

**Q: Does a longer warranty mean better furniture?**
