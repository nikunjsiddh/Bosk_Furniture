# How to Choose the Right Wardrobe for Your Bedroom

A wardrobe is one of the hardest-working pieces of furniture in your home — and one of the most expensive to get wrong. Choose well and it organises your life for the next 15 years. Choose poorly and you're left with doors that bang into the bed, shelves in the wrong places and storage you can't actually reach. Here's how to choose the right wardrobe for your bedroom, step by step.

### Step 1: Start with the space, not the wardrobe

Before anything else, measure. Note the wall width, the ceiling height, and where doors, windows and switches fall. The single biggest advantage of a fitted wardrobe is that it uses your space completely — floor to ceiling, wall to wall — so there are no dust-collecting gaps on top and no wasted corners. Standard readymade wardrobes almost never achieve this.

### Step 2: Sliding or hinged doors?

This is the first real decision, and it depends on your room.

**Hinged (openable) doors**
- Give you **full access** to the whole wardrobe at once.
- Allow **more internal accessories** on the back of the doors.
- Often **more affordable**.
- **Need clearance** in front to swing open — not ideal for tight rooms.

**Sliding doors**
- **Save floor space** — nothing swings out, so they're perfect for compact bedrooms or where the bed is close.
- Look **sleek and modern**, especially with mirrored or laminated panels.
- You can only access **half the wardrobe at a time**.
- Run on tracks, so **good-quality hardware is essential** for smooth, long-lasting movement.

**Rule of thumb:** tight room or bed close to the wardrobe → sliding. Plenty of clearance and you want full access → hinged.

### Step 3: Get the internal layout right

A wardrobe is only as good as its insides. Plan around what you actually own:

- **Long-hanging section** for dresses, kurtas and coats.
- **Double-hanging** (two rails stacked) for shirts and folded-length garments — doubles your hanging capacity.
- **Shelves** for folded clothes, with at least a couple that are adjustable.
- **Drawers** for innerwear, accessories and valuables (add a soft-close lockable drawer for jewellery).
- **Dedicated zones** for bags, shoes or a small safe if you need them.

Tip: think about *who* uses the wardrobe. A couple's wardrobe needs more hanging space; a child's wardrobe needs lower, reachable shelves that adapt as they grow.

### Step 4: Choose the right material

Your bedroom is usually a dry zone, but a wardrobe is a long-term investment, so material quality decides how long it stays straight and solid. Look for furniture built on **quality plywood** rather than particle board — particle board can sag and swell over time, especially in humid climates. ### Step 5: Don't ignore the hardware

The parts you can't see decide how the wardrobe feels every single day. Quality **hinges, sliding channels and soft-close mechanisms** are what separate a wardrobe that glides shut silently for a decade from one that starts sticking and squeaking within months. BOSK uses **Hettich** German hardware — backed by a 10+ year warranty — for exactly this reason.

### Step 6: Pick a finish that lasts and suits the room

- **Laminate** — durable, huge range of colours and wood textures, easy to maintain. The practical favourite.
- **Acrylic** — high-gloss, premium look (shows fingerprints more).
- **Veneer** — natural wood warmth for a luxury feel.
- **Mirrored panels** — great on sliding doors; they save you a separate mirror and make the room feel bigger.

Match the finish to your flooring and bed for a coordinated look, or use a mirrored/handle-less front for a clean, modern bedroom.

## FAQ (add as FAQ schema if possible)

**Q: Are sliding or hinged wardrobes better?**
A: Sliding doors save floor space and suit compact rooms, while hinged doors give full access and more internal accessory options. Choose sliding for tight spaces and hinged where you have clearance to open doors fully.

**Q: What material is best for a bedroom wardrobe?**
A: Quality plywood is the best base — it stays straight and resists sagging better than particle board. Marine-grade (IS:710) plywood, which BOSK uses, offers the highest durability and moisture resistance.

**Q: How do I plan the inside of my wardrobe?**
A: Plan around what you own — combine long-hanging and double-hanging sections, adjustable shelves, drawers for small items, and dedicated zones for bags or shoes. A custom maker can tailor the layout to your needs.

**Q: How much hanging space do I need?**
A: It depends on your wardrobe — but adding a double-hanging section (two rails stacked) effectively doubles capacity for shirts and shorter garments, which suits most users.

**Q: Is a custom wardrobe better than a readymade one?**
A: Yes, for most bedrooms — a custom wardrobe fits your exact wall and ceiling height, uses all available space, and lets you choose materials, finish and internal layout.
